EventOwl formulas: R multiple, MAE, MFE, event tags and statistics

Every number EventOwl shows, and exactly how it is calculated, for engine version 1.00. The same definitions appear behind the i icons in the panel, and every input is in the audit CSV, so you can repeat any calculation. All times in the history are trade server time; the panel converts them to UTC with a server time model. Money is in the account currency.

0. Server time model

  • Default model "NY+7": server time = New York time + 7 h.
    • That is GMT+2 when the US is on standard time and GMT+3 during US daylight saving time.
    • US DST runs from the 2nd Sunday of March 07:00 UTC to the 1st Sunday of November 06:00 UTC.
    • This was verified for IC Markets, including the weeks when US and EU clocks differ.
  • Other models: fixed offset, or "EU DST" (base offset + 1 h from the last Sunday of March 01:00 UTC to the last Sunday of October 01:00 UTC).
  • Calendar times: The MT5 economic calendar returns event times shifted by the server's offset at the moment of reading. Event UTC = calendar time − current server offset. The current offset is round((TimeTradeServer() − TimeGMT()) / 30 min) and is used only when it matches the model.

1. Trades from deals

  • Grouping: Deals of type BUY or SELL are grouped by DEAL_POSITION_ID.
  • Direction: The type of the first entry deal sets the direction.
  • Entry price: Volume-weighted average of the entry deals.
  • Exit price: Volume-weighted average of the exit deals (OUT and OUT_BY).
  • Times: Entry time = first entry deal. Exit time = last exit deal.
  • Partial closes: Belong to one trade.
  • Netting reversal (INOUT):
    • The deal closes the open volume and opens the rest in the opposite direction. This becomes two trades: <id> and <id>-2.
    • The deal's profit goes to the closing part.
    • Its swap, commission and fee are split by volume.
  • Separate charge deals: Other deals that carry the same position id (e.g. a commission charged as a separate deal) are added to that trade.
  • Net result = profit + swap + commission + fee.
  • Open positions: Shown, but never counted in statistics.

2. Initial risk, 1R

  • SL: The SL of the first entry deal (DEAL_SL, which MT5 copies from the opening order).
    • MT5 does not store later SL changes.
    • The SL on the exit deal is the SL at the moment of closing, so it is not used.
  • 1R money = |loss| if the first entry volume had been closed at that SL. Formula: OrderCalcProfit(type, symbol, first volume, first entry price, SL).
    • The conversion uses current market rates. For USD-quoted symbols on a USD account (XAUUSD, USTEC, BTCUSD) it is exact. For crosses and JPY pairs it can differ slightly from the historical value.
    • If OrderCalcProfit fails, tick value × ticks is used and the trade carries the flag risk_from_tick_value.
    • A result of 0 for a non-zero move means MetaTrader has no quotes yet (for example right after the terminal starts). It is treated as unknown, never as 0. A trade with a stop loss but no priced 1R carries the flag risk_no_price_data. The panel waits up to 60 s for the terminal to connect before it calculates.
  • No SL on the entry deal:
    • If the panel recorded the SL later (forward journal), that SL is used (flag sl_from_journal).
    • Otherwise the user's fallback applies: a fixed money amount, or a % of the balance at entry. The trade is then marked r_estimate.
    • With no fallback the trade has no R.
  • Balance at entry = sum of all deal results (deposits included) before the entry moment.
  • R multiple = net result / 1R money.

3. MAE and MFE

  • Excursions (in price units, never negative):
    • LONG: MAE = entry − lowest price, MFE = highest price − entry.
    • SHORT: MAE = highest price − entry, MFE = entry − lowest price.
  • From M1 bars, in both editions:
    • Bars from the minute of entry to the minute of exit, both included.
    • LONG uses bid bars.
    • SHORT uses ask approximated as bid + the bar's spread (flag short_ask_from_bar_spread).
    • The first and last bars include prices from before entry and after exit, so M1 values are approximate (flag excursion_m1_approx).
  • No tick option: MAE and MFE always come from M1 bars. Ticks are used only for the spread at entry and the slippage, in EventOwl Pro (section 4).
  • Money: The price move applied to the total volume from the weighted entry price, through the same conversion as 1R.
  • In R: MAE money / 1R money.

4. Spread at entry

  • From ticks (EventOwl Pro, recent trades): ask − bid of the tick nearest to the entry moment, within 60 s. If two ticks are equally near, the earlier one wins.
  • Without ticks: The M1 bar's spread × point (flag spread_from_bar).
  • Two passes: The engine first fills every trade from M1 data (fast). In EventOwl Pro a second pass then replaces the spread at entry and the market-order slippage (section 5) with tick-based values where ticks exist, for trades entered in the last N days (input, default 30; 0 = all). It runs newest trades first, in the background, with progress shown. A tick request costs about 0.03–0.1 s in MT5. Until a trade is refined it keeps the M1-based value and its flag. MAE and MFE are not refined; they stay from M1 bars (section 3). EventOwl (free) uses the M1 values only.

5. Slippage

  • Sign: Positive = worse for the trader. Buy: fill − reference. Sell: reference − fill.
  • Pending orders: The reference is the order price.
  • Market orders:
    • If the broker stored a requested price, that is the reference.
    • Otherwise the reference is the quote at the order's setup time (ask for buy, bid for sell; flag slippage_vs_quote). IC Markets stores 0 for market orders.
    • That quote comes from ticks, so it exists only for trades refined from ticks (section 4). Otherwise the slippage is unknown (flag slippage_no_data).

6. Event tags

  • Which events: Calendar events whose time is in [moment − X, moment + Y] for the entry or the exit (default X = Y = 15 min).
  • Major events (default): Of the high-importance events, only those whose code is a central bank rate decision or press/news conference, a Fed chair appearance, nonfarm payrolls with average hourly earnings, CPI (any variant), PCE price index, GDP, retail sales, unemployment rate or employment change. ADP, PMI, ISM, JOLTS, jobless claims, PPI, sentiment surveys, bond auctions and oil stocks are left out even when the calendar marks them high. The input "Events to show and tag" switches to every high-importance event.
  • Prop firm news rules (Pro): Prop Guard news windows always use every high-importance event, because firms define news more widely.
  • Currencies: The symbol's fiat currencies: base and profit currency, if they are fiat. XAUUSD, USTEC and BTCUSD → USD. EURUSD → EUR and USD.
  • Matching: By the language-independent event code, not by the name; the name comes in the terminal language.
  • Label: Currency + short label, e.g. "USD NFP", "USD CPI", "USD FOMC".
  • Other events: A readable title made from the code, e.g. "USD EIA Crude Oil Stocks Change"; period suffixes (m/m, y/y, q/q) are dropped.
  • Same minute: Events in the same minute appear once per label. CPI m/m, y/y and core all become one "USD CPI".
  • Label order: A trade's labels are listed main release first, in this order: rate decision, press conference, NFP, CPI, GDP, PCE, retail sales, employment change, unemployment rate, hourly earnings, Fed chair; other events follow by time. The trade list shows the first label and "+N" for the others, for example "USD NFP +2".
  • Audit CSV: Records the event times in server time, the offset in minutes from the matched moment, and which side matched (entry, exit, or both).
  • Calendar unavailable: If the calendar does not answer (e.g. error 5401 on some networks), event tags are off and the panel says so.

7. Session

Label of the entry time, using local clocks with their own daylight saving rules:

SessionLocal hours
AsiaTokyo 09:00–18:00
LondonLondon 08:00–17:00
New YorkNew York 08:00–17:00
  • Precedence: London-NY overlap > London > New York > Asia > Off-hours.
  • Crypto weekend: Crypto symbols get "Weekend" from Friday 17:00 to Sunday 17:00 New York time.

8. Fix windows

  • Windows: Tokyo 09:55 JST and London 16:00 London time, ±10 min, on weekdays.
  • Tagging: A trade is tagged when its entry or exit falls inside the window. Holding through a fix is not tagged.

9. User tags

  • Sources:
    • A keyword found in the entry comment (case-insensitive). Pro input: "news, breakout"; the tag is the keyword.
    • A magic number. Pro input: "12345=Breakout, 777"; the tag is the name, or "magic:777".
    • Manual tags typed in the trade detail popup (Pro, account history only), stored in MQL5\Files\EventOwl\<server>_<login>_tags.txt when you press Enter. Comma separated; a "|" inside a tag becomes "/".
  • Duplicates: Removed.

10. Query statistics

  • Filters: symbol, direction, event, session, fix, tag, weekday and hour of entry (server time). Only closed trades are counted.
  • Event, fix and tag filters:
    • "any": no filter.
    • "*": any non-empty value.
    • "-": none.
    • Or a specific value.
  • Outcome: Win if R > 0.05, loss if R < −0.05, else breakeven. Without R, the sign of the net result is used.
  • Hit rate = wins / N.
  • Mean R, median R: Over trades with R. Expected R = mean R.
  • 95% CI of mean R: Percentile bootstrap, 2,000 resamples, fixed seed, so it is reproducible.
  • Max MAE: Largest MAE in R and in money.
  • Longest losing streak: Consecutive losses by exit time; a breakeven ends the streak.
  • Total net: Sum of net results.
  • Estimated R: Trades with an estimated 1R are counted and shown separately. They can be excluded.
  • Sample warnings:
    • N < 30: "small sample".
    • Fewer than 10 trades with R: no mean, median or CI; the raw list only.

11. Reconcile

  • Balance check: Sum of every deal's profit + swap + commission + fee (deposits included) vs. ACCOUNT_BALANCE. With the full history selected the difference must be 0.00.
  • Split: The sum is split into trades and other deals (deposits, withdrawals, credits, unattached charges). This lets it be compared with the MT5 History tab.

12. Pro: Big R finder

  • Trades used: Closed trades with a usable R (R estimates can be excluded).
  • Group: The top (or bottom) 10% or 20% of these trades by R, at least 1 trade: group size = ceil(fraction x N).
  • Threshold: The R of the last trade in the group ("R at least" for winners, "R at most" for losers).
  • Labels per trade: Event, session, fix, symbol, direction, weekday (server time of entry), hour of entry, user tag. A trade with two events or tags counts once for each.
  • Share in group = trades of the group with the label / group size.
  • Share of all = trades with the label / N.
  • Lift = share in group / share of all. Lift above 1: the label is more common among the biggest winners (or losers) than in general.
  • Listing: Labels with fewer than 2 trades in the group are not listed. Labels on every trade (lift always 1) are not listed. Sorted by lift, then by count in the group. Labels with fewer than 5 trades in the group are shown in yellow: their lift is unreliable.
  • It describes past trades; it does not say why.

13. Pro: Replay and reshuffles

  • R sequence: The R values of the selected closed trades, ordered by exit time.
  • Replay: Start at 100. After each trade: equity x (1 + risk% x R). If equity reaches 0, it stays 0 ("ruin").
  • Max drawdown: The largest fall from a running peak, in % of that peak.
  • Longest losing streak: Most consecutive trades with R below -0.05 (the breakeven band).
  • Reshuffles: 10,000 random orders of the same R values (Fisher-Yates shuffle, xorshift64* with a fixed seed, so the numbers repeat). For each order: max drawdown and longest losing streak.
  • Percentiles: Value at position ceil(p x 10,000) of the sorted results (median, 90%, 95%, 99%).
  • Limit: Share of orders whose max drawdown reached the chosen limit. "Equity reached 0": share of orders with ruin.
  • Title on screen: "Replay of your past trades. Not a forecast." Reshuffles reuse your own R values; they do not model future trades.

14. Pro: Prop Guard news window

  • Rule: N minutes before and M minutes after a news event; applies to the open and/or the close of a trade; a winning trade in the window has its profit removed, or counted at X%.
  • Where the rule comes from: The minutes come from the firm preset you pick (section 22) or from the inputs, and you can change the rule in the panel. 0 minutes before and 0 minutes after means no news rule.
  • Events: High-importance events of the MT5 economic calendar for the symbol's currencies (same mapping as section 6), times corrected to UTC with the server time model.
  • In the window: event - N min <= trade moment <= event + M min, checked for the open and/or the close.
  • Shown per trade: the nearest matching event, its server time, "trade - event" in minutes (negative = before the news), and the side (open, close, open+close).
  • Profit at risk: For a trade in the window with net > 0: net (rule "removed"), or net x (1 - X/100) (rule "counts at X%"). Losing trades are listed but not changed.
  • Net after the rule = total net of all closed trades - profit at risk.
  • Firms may use other news sources and times.

15. Pro: Strategy Tester report import

  • File: HTML report saved from the Strategy Tester, copied to MQL5\Files\EventOwl\import\. UTF-16 and UTF-8 are read.
  • Deals table: time, deal, symbol, type, direction (in, out, in/out, out by), volume, price, order, commission, swap, profit, balance, comment.
  • Orders table: the stop loss of each order. The SL of the order that opened a position is its initial SL (1R).
  • Position ids: The report has none. Positions are matched first in, first out per symbol; an exit deal that closes several positions is split by volume (profit, commission and swap in proportion).
  • Reconcile: The sum of all deals (deposit included) vs. the report's final balance; the report's totals row is also checked.
  • Every imported trade carries the flag simulated_tester_report. The simulated set is never mixed with the account history. No tick refinement.

16. Pro: share card

  • PNG, 1200 x 630, of the current query: N, wins/losses/breakeven, hit rate, mean R with its 95% range, median R, max MAE in R (with how many trades have MAE data), longest losing streak, a histogram of R in 0.5R buckets from -3R to +5R (values outside go to the end buckets), and the date range.
  • No account number, broker or money amounts. Footer: "N = ... closed trades. Past results, not a forecast." A simulated set is marked "SIMULATED".
  • Written as PNG by the panel itself; no external library or service is used.

17. Pro: Event Radar

  • Events: Events of the MT5 economic calendar for the next 8 days: the major events of section 6 by default, or every high-importance event by input. Currencies come from the chart symbol and the open positions (same mapping as section 6). Times are converted to UTC as in section 0 and shown in server time. Reloaded every 30 minutes, or when the currencies change.
  • Same minute: Releases of one currency in the same minute are named after the main release, in the order of section 6 (for example "USD NFP", not "USD Earnings"). Releases with the same label (for example CPI m/m and y/y) are listed once.
  • Your past trades near an event: The query of section 10 with the event label as the event filter: once for the chart symbol, once for all symbols. "Near" means the event tag of section 6 (entry or exit within the event window). Mean R is shown only from 10 trades with R. While a simulated report is loaded (section 15), the alerts and the one-line view of the minimized panel do not use its trades.
  • Alerts (optional): A terminal alert:
    • the chosen minutes before an event;
    • the chosen minutes before your news window (section 14) starts, when a position of a matching currency is open;
    • when 80% of the daily loss limit is used;
    • 30 minutes before the FX week closes (Friday 17:00 New York time), when positions are open and weekend holding is not allowed. Each alert is shown once.
  • Phone (optional, off by default): With the input "Also send the alerts to your phone", every alert is also put in a queue and sent with MetaTrader's own push notifications. They need your MetaQuotes ID in Tools > Options > Notifications. MetaTrader limits push notifications, so the panel sends at most one every 1.5 seconds and 9 per minute. A push that fails is tried again. Alerts of one release minute and currency are merged into one push. If notifications are switched off in MetaTrader, the panel shows a status message. The terminal alert itself is the same with the input on or off.
  • Past results, not a forecast.

18. Pro: Rule simulator

  • Trades used: Closed trades with R, in entry order.
  • Filters (remove trades):
    • Entry within ±N minutes of an event of the event tags (section 6: major events by default, or every high-importance event by input); only the open is checked.
    • A fix tag.
    • Chosen sessions or weekdays (server time of entry).
    • More than N entries in a server day (the first N are kept).
    • Entries after N losing trades of the same day have closed.
  • Stop to entry at +X R:
    • The price at +X R is entry ± X × (first entry price − initial stop loss).
    • If the M1 path reached it, and from the following bar on came back to the entry price before exit, the trade's R becomes its costs only: (commission + fee) / 1R.
    • Inside one bar the order of high and low is unknown, so the return is looked for from the bar after the trigger.
  • Close half at +X R:
    • If the trade's MFE reached X R, half of it counts X R and the other half its real result (or the stop-to-entry result).
  • Statistics: Section 10, plus:
    • total R;
    • max drawdown of a 1% replay (section 13);
    • mean R of the first and second half of the trades in time.
  • Each rule on its own: The same, with only that rule. "Better in both halves" means mean R is higher than without the rule in both halves.
  • Caveat: In-sample, so the rules are tested on the trades that suggested them. With several rules tried, a difference inside the 95% range can be chance. Not a forecast.

19. Pro: Prop Guard

  • Rules: From the firm preset you pick (section 22: firm, program and stage, with the date the rules were read) or, with no preset, from the numbers you enter in the inputs: account size (or the first deposit), daily loss %, max loss % (static or trailing), the hour the trading day starts (server time), consistency %, risk per trade %, no weekend holding. With a preset, an input is used only where section 22 says so. The news window is section 14.
  • Daily loss, history:
    • The closed net result of each trading day is compared with −(daily loss % × account size).
    • The first trade that crosses it is listed.
    • Floating losses during a day are not in the history.
  • Daily loss, live:
    • Used = closed balance at the start of the trading day − equity now (0 when equity is above the day-start balance).
    • Left = limit − used.
  • Max loss:
    • Static floor = account size − max loss % × size.
    • Trailing floor = highest balance − max loss % × size.
    • History compares the closed balance after each trade; live compares equity.
  • Consistency: Top day (the largest net result of a trading day) / total net profit, compared with the limit (only when the total is positive).
  • Risk per trade:
    • History: 1R of a trade (section 2) / account size. R estimates are not counted.
    • Live: the money at an open position's stop loss / account size.
  • Weekend holding: A position open over a Saturday (server time).
  • Minimum holding time: From presets only; there is no input. History lists each trade whose time from entry to exit (section 1) is shorter than the preset's minimum.
  • Measurement differences: Firms measure in different ways (for example the higher of balance and equity at the day start, or another reset time). The panel says so and never closes positions.

20. What stands out

  • Groups: Every group of the breakdown dimensions in the edition (free: session, symbol, direction; Pro: also event and fix) with at least 10 closed trades with R. A dimension with only one group (for example a single symbol) is skipped.
  • Lines: The group with the highest mean R (if above 0) and the one with the lowest (if below 0), each with its 95% range (400 bootstrap resamples), trade count and hit rate. If the range includes 0, the line says the difference can be chance.
  • Events: Mean R with its 95% range (400 bootstrap resamples) and trade count of the trades near a major event, next to the same for the other trades, when both groups have at least 10 trades with R. "Near" means an event tag of section 6; with the input set to every high-importance event, the line compares those instead. When the two ranges overlap, the line says the difference can be chance.
  • Nothing to say: With fewer trades the card says how many trades with R there are.

21. Pro: News reaction

  • Releases: Major events (or every high-importance event, by input) of the chart symbol's currencies in the last 400 days, at least one hour old.
  • Clusters: Releases of one currency in the same minute are one release, named after the highest-ranked one, in the order of section 6.
  • Moves (M5 bars, server time): p0 = open of the M5 bar at the release time (floored to 5 minutes). 5 min = close of that bar − p0; 15 min = close of the bar 10 minutes later − p0; 60 min = close of the bar 55 minutes later − p0. Range = highest high − lowest low of the first three bars. A missing bar leaves the value unknown.
  • Units: Pips for 3- and 5-digit symbols, otherwise price in the symbol's profit currency.
  • Better / worse than expected: The calendar's impact flag for the currency (majority of the highest-ranked releases in the cluster). Above / below: actual against forecast, from the month-on-month figure where there is one.
  • Past tendency: "Mostly up" or "mostly down" only when one direction has at least 70% of at least 6 releases with a known 15-minute move; otherwise "mixed" or "too few".
  • Not a forecast: These are past reactions. The next release can move the other way.

22. Pro: Prop firm presets

  • Data: One row per firm, program and stage (and purchase-date cohort or size band where the rules differ), read from each firm's own pages. Every row keeps its source, the date it was read and a confidence level. The data ships inside the EA; it is not downloaded.
  • Source on screen: The panel names the source as the firm and the date the rules were read, without a web address.
  • Mapping: Daily loss %, max loss % (static or trailing), the day reset, news window minutes, consistency %, risk per trade %, minimum holding time and the weekend rule.
  • Day reset: Converted to server time for each day from the firm's clock: Europe/Prague, or New York (for example 17:00), each with its daylight saving rules. "EST" in a firm's terms is taken as New York time with daylight saving. A reset on the firm's own MT5 server clock keeps the firm's hour, unless you set the day start in the inputs.
  • Inputs with a preset: Where the preset has no number (an unknown daily loss) or the firm's day reset is on its own server clock, the panel uses the matching input if you set one, and says so.
  • Evaluation stages: Rules that the firm applies only to funded accounts (news window, consistency) are not checked in evaluation stages, that is stages whose name contains Phase, Challenge, Step, Evaluation, Level 1 or single-day. The note says so.
  • News window without minutes: If the firm does not define its news window in minutes, your own window (section 14) is kept.
  • One trade's share of the profit: A rule on how much of the total profit a single trade may make is shown as a note, not checked.
  • Where the panel measures differently, it says so: a daily limit from the higher of balance and equity, from the day's highest equity or as a percent of the day-start value; a trailing floor that stops at the initial balance or never stops; a trailing floor that follows equity, which can be closer than the panel shows; where a news rule applies and what happens on a breach; a minimum holding time that is an average, or only affects the profit share.
  • Account size: From the input, else the first deposit.
  • No preset: The rules come from the inputs.

Known limitations

  • M1 approximation: MAE/MFE from M1 is approximate (edge bars; SHORT ask from bar spread).
  • M1 depth: The terminal keeps M1 bars only back to a limit (Tools > Options > Charts > Max bars in chart; 100,000 bars is about 4 to 5 months). Older trades show no MAE/MFE and are counted as "no price data". After 3 empty requests in a row for a symbol, older trades of that symbol are not requested again until Refresh.
  • Tick depth: Tick data at IC Markets reaches about 6 months back for USTEC/BTCUSD.
  • Conversion rates: 1R and excursion money use current conversion rates for non-USD-quoted symbols.
  • Unrecorded SLs: An SL set after entry is unknown unless the panel was running.
  • Calendar network: The economic calendar may not be reachable on some networks; event tags are then off.
  • Strategy Tester: The calendar is not available in the Strategy Tester.
